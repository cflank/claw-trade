Let me respond directly to the Aggressive Analyst, because I think you're conflating hope with strategy, and that is exactly how firms blow through their risk limits.

You say the China trip is an asymmetric setup where the upside is massive and the downside is already priced in. That is not analysis—that is speculation dressed up as conviction. Let me ask you: what if the trip goes poorly? What if Trump and Xi fail to agree on anything, or worse, what if new tariffs are announced on consumer electronics? You say the downside is "already priced in," but AAPL is at $292, just off its all-time high, with a P/E of 35.5x on 3% revenue growth. That is not a stock pricing in catastrophe. That is a stock pricing in perfection. If the China outcome disappoints, that gap-up you're hoping for becomes a gap-down, and you're caught buying at the high with no margin of safety. "Buy before the catalyst" is a casino bet, not a risk-managed trade.

You dismiss the insider selling as portfolio rebalancing. But $71 million is not a trivial amount, and Arthur Levinson is not a retiree cashing out for a beach house—he is the chairman of the board. He has access to every piece of non-public information about Apple's operations, supply chain, and regulatory risks. If you want to bet against that signal, be my guest, but I will trust the person who actually sits in the boardroom over a Wall Street analyst with a price target. And yes, Apple buys back $66 billion, but that is a capital return program designed to offset dilution and boost EPS, not a real-time vote of confidence at every price level. The board authorizes buybacks at $200. They also authorize them at $290. The question is whether $292 is a good price, not whether buybacks exist.

Your technical argument is the most dangerous. You say RSI at 72 is not a sell signal in a breakout trend. Historically, that is true—sometimes. But the key word is "sometimes." The RSI has been above 70 for exactly two days. That is a fresh overbought reading, not an entrenched momentum regime. And you are ignoring the Bollinger Band proximity. The stock is $1.35 below the upper band. In a strong uptrend, price can ride the band, but when it touches the band on a $6.47 ATR, the probability of mean reversion increases significantly. The MACD is accelerating, yes, but that acceleration is already priced into the $292 level. You are buying after the move, not before it. The golden cross you cite is a lagging indicator—it confirmed a trend that started weeks ago.

Your fundamental defense of the valuation is simply incorrect. A 30.6x forward P/E on 16% expected EPS growth gives a PEG of 1.9, not 2.57 as reported, but that 16% growth is the key assumption. What if that growth doesn't materialize? Apple's revenue grew 3% in the most recent quarter. The EPS growth of 16% is entirely driven by buybacks and margin expansion, not organic top-line acceleration. If the macro environment weakens—and Barron's explicitly warned this week that high oil prices are a "time bomb for the tech-led rally"—that revenue growth could slip to 1-2%, and EPS growth drops to 8-10%. At that point, a 30x forward multiple becomes untenable. You are paying for growth that is entirely dependent on financial engineering and cost cuts, not genuine business momentum.

The Neutral Analyst says "hold with a bias toward accumulating on weakness." You call that indecision. I call it discipline. The difference between 4% and a full position is the difference between sleeping well at night and waking up to a gap-down in a volatile tape. You want to buy half now and sell puts at $285. That is a reasonable strategy for an aggressive trader. But the firm's mandate is to protect assets and minimize volatility. I am not saying never buy Apple. I am saying do not buy at $292 with a binary geopolitical event, overbought technicals, insider selling, and a macro environment that is explicitly flagged as vulnerable. The risk of a 5-10% drawdown from here is materially higher than the probability of a gap-up to $310. And that 5-10% drawdown could happen in a single session if the China talks turn sour.

You say passive is the riskiest position of all. I say active without a risk framework is simply gambling. The safe, prudent, and ultimately more profitable move is to wait. Let the China trip pass. Let the RSI cool. Buy the dip at $283 or $275. If the stock gaps up to $310, we missed the first leg—so what? There will be another entry. Apple is not going to zero. But if we buy here and the stock drops to $270, we have to explain to the risk committee why we ignored every warning signal in the playbook. I will take the "missed opportunity" over the "explained loss" every single time.
