# 投资辩论（多头观点）

Thank you for joining me today. Let me address the bear case head-on, starting with the elephant in the room: the RSI at 82.08 and the stretched distance above the 200-day moving average.

Yes, I see those numbers. I've been an analyst long enough to know that overbought readings can make people nervous. But let me show you why this time is different, and why the fundamental picture completely overwhelms the technical noise.

**On Valuation:** The bear says 36.2x trailing earnings is "stretched." But look at what you're getting for that multiple. Apple generated *$101 billion in free cash flow* over the trailing twelve months. That's not a company struggling for growth – that's a cash-printing machine with a 27.2% net profit margin that's still expanding. Evercore ISI just raised their price target to $365 with a bull case of $500. They're not guessing – they're watching the Services revenue compound at over 15% annually with gross margins above 70%. When a business shifts from selling hardware at 49% margins to selling subscriptions at 70%+ margins, the multiple *should* expand.

**On the CEO Transition:** The bear points to Tim Cook stepping down as uncertainty. I'd argue it's the opposite. John Ternus is the architect of the M-series chip transition – arguably Apple's most successful strategic move since the iPhone. An internal successor who understands the silicon strategy and the Services flywheel? That's continuity, not disruption. Cook himself was an internal successor, and we all know how that worked out.

**On the Technical Picture:** The 50-day SMA just crossed above the 200-day SMA – a golden cross that remains intact. The MACD is in full acceleration mode with the histogram expanding to 1.95, and it hasn't reached the extremes we saw before past corrections. Volume is confirming the move – 79.9 million shares on the May 1 breakout versus a 37 million average. That's institutional accumulation, not retail speculation. Yes, the RSI is high, but it was above 70 for five straight weeks in late 2025 while the stock kept climbing. Overbought can stay overbought in a structural uptrend.

**On KeyBanc's Caution:** Let me address this directly. KeyBanc says they see "initial cracks" in hardware demand. Fine. But the bull case for Apple has never been about iPhone unit growth – it's about the *installed base monetization*. There are over 2 billion active Apple devices. Every one of them generates Services revenue. When hardware sales slow, Apple doesn't panic – they raise their Services prices, which they've done consistently. The Services segment is now a >$100 billion annual revenue stream growing at 15%+. That's the compounding engine the bear is underestimating.

**On the China Risk:** The Trump-Xi summit is a binary event, yes. But Apple has spent the last five years diversifying supply chains to India and Vietnam. They're less dependent on China today than they were in 2021. And if trade tensions de-escalate – which the market is pricing in given the VIX collapse to 17 – Apple's China revenue (~18% of total) gets a direct boost.

**On the Google "Revenge" Narrative:** This is the weakest bear argument. Google has been "going to beat Apple" for 15 years. Meanwhile, Apple's ecosystem has gotten stickier, their privacy-focused AI strategy is differentiating them in enterprise, and their installed base continues to grow. Google's Android may have global market share, but Apple captures 85% of smartphone industry profits. That's not a moat that's narrowing – that's a fortress.

**The Bottom Line:** This is a company with $451 billion in trailing revenue, $101 billion in free cash flow, a 27.2% net margin, a 141.5% ROE, and a stock that's 22% below Evercore's base target and 67% below their bull case. Yes, the technicals are extended. But the fundamentals are accelerating.

My recommendation: Hold existing positions. For new money, buy the pullback to $285-290. But don't let a high RSI scare you out of a structural bull market in the world's best cash flow generator. The Services compounding thesis is real, the capital returns are unmatched, and the leadership transition is orderly.
