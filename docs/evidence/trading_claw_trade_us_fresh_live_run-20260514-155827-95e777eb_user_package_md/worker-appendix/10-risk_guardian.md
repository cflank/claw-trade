# 风险辩论（防守方）

Let me address the aggressive analyst’s position directly, because this kind of reckless optimism is how firms get burned.

You’re calling my analysis “paralysis” while advocating for buying a stock at technically extreme levels, into a binary geopolitical event, during a CEO transition, with a valuation that historically has preceded significant drawdowns. That’s not conviction—that’s gambling with firm capital.

Let’s start with your dismissal of the valuation concern. You say a 36.2x P/E and a 2.58 PEG ratio are somehow irrelevant because of Apple’s free cash flow. I’ve read the fundamentals report too. That $101 billion in TTM free cash flow is impressive—but at $299 per share, you’re still paying 43 times that cash flow. The forward P/E of 31.2x is not cheap; it’s priced for perfection. And perfection is exactly what the current price reflects. You’re hanging your entire thesis on Evercore ISI’s $500 bull case. But one sell-side analyst’s dream scenario is not a trading plan. KeyBanc, a real institutional research desk, is literally telling you their proprietary data shows “initial cracks” in hardware demand. You call that “seasonal normality.” I call it a warning signal that the foundational assumption of your Services thesis—hardware unit growth driving ecosystem expansion—is showing stress. If those “cracks” widen, the 31x forward P/E becomes 35x on lowered estimates. That’s how corrections happen.

Now your technical argument. You say RSI at 82 is not a sell signal in a secular bull market. Let me quote the technical analysis report directly: “Every time RSI has exceeded 80 in this bull cycle, a meaningful pullback followed within 1-3 weeks.” This is not my opinion; this is the data showing three consecutive instances of this exact pattern. You correctly note that after those pullbacks, the stock eventually made new highs. But here’s what you conveniently ignore: those pullbacks were 6-10% declines. A 6% decline from $299 is $281. A 10% decline is $269. The trader’s plan calls for a re-entry at $250-$260. That’s not “fantasy”—that’s the actual historical behavior of this stock when it hits these technical extremes. You’re telling the firm to hold through a 6-10% decline because you assume the Services thesis will eventually bail you out. That’s not risk management; that’s hope-based investing. We don’t manage client assets on hope.

Your handling of the Trump-Xi summit and CEO transition is the most concerning part of your argument. You say the market is “pricing a positive outcome.” I say the VIX at 17 is not pricing in the binary risk of a breakdown in trade talks that would directly hit Apple’s China revenue and supply chain. You say Ternus’s succession is “a non-event.” I say any CEO transition at a $4.4 trillion company introduces execution risk, regardless of how competent the successor appears. Tim Cook was irreplaceable for a decade; pretending otherwise is naive. The social sentiment report itself explicitly identifies “heightened uncertainty” around this transition. You’re dismissing the very risks the firm pays analysts to identify.

Let me address your specific risk/reward calculation. You claim a 26% upside to $365 for a 6% risk to $279. That math only works if $279 holds. But what happens if the Trump-Xi talks collapse and the stock gaps down to $275 intraday? Your stop at $279 gets breached in the first hour, and you’ve locked in a loss while the stock continues to fall. The $250-$260 re-entry target isn’t a “falling knife” fantasy—it’s a level with multiple technical supports: the 50-day SMA at $265, the 200-day SMA at $258, and the historical pullback levels from the last three RSI >80 events. That’s where the risk/reward actually becomes favorable for re-entry.

You accuse me of “selling the narrative because the price looks perfect.” I’ll reverse that on you: you’re buying the narrative because the momentum feels good. That’s the mistake past traders made at every intermediate top in this cycle. The plan to sell into strength and wait for a better entry is not paralysis—it’s discipline. It’s incorporating the lesson from “past mistakes—avoiding the trap of overvaluing a strong narrative when the price already reflects perfection.”

The conservative position is clear: sell the full position into strength, protect the firm’s capital from the binary risk of the summit and the technical exhaustion signals, and wait for a re-entry at $250-$260 where the risk/reward shifts back in our favor. That’s how you compound capital over decades, not by chasing momentum into an overbought extreme with three known risk factors converging simultaneously.
